Concept Research

Property buying parameters: customer research findings

Three combined survey instruments testing how customers perceive the value of saving their property buying parameters, what they would call this set of parameters in their own words, and which name they pick when shown a list.

The "BuyBox" term itself was tested separately in a prior Amplitude survey. That research established that approximately 80% of participants either had not heard the term "BuyBox" or did not know what it meant. This survey does not re-test the term. It focuses on the underlying concept: the perceived value, the language users reach for unprompted, and which alternative labels customers select when presented with options.

157 Total unique respondents across the combined surveys (after removing unqualified from screener #1)
118 Open-ended responses on what they would call this concept
117 Respondents who selected one or more labels from the provided list

02 / Perceived value

How useful does the concept feel to customers?

Respondents were shown a description of a saved set of property buying parameters that the platform would use to surface matching properties, then rated its usefulness and explained why. "Imagine a real estate auction site lets you save information about the kinds of properties you are most interested in. How useful would this be to you?"

Verbatim · why this would be useful
  • It would allow me to digitize my portfolio of potential properties while utilizing tools that I may not have access to already.
  • It would be very helpful to have all the information in one place.
  • This would be very helpful so I wouldn't have to enter the information each time I log in. It would just show exactly what I was looking for.
  • It sounds like a one stop shop. You set up your preferences, monitor, and easy peasy.
  • It's useful because Auction.com can instantly surface properties that fit your exact criteria instead of making you sift through irrelevant listings.
  • This kind of information is very useful if you're serious about investing because it helps narrow down properties that actually fit your goals instead of wasting time on bad deals.
Method. The usefulness rating was asked in one of the three surveys (n = 21). Three response options were available: Extremely useful, Somewhat useful, Not useful.
